What Muse is, in plain words

Muse is a personal assistant that lives in an app on your phone or computer. You type (or say) what you need in normal, everyday language — and it answers, writes, searches, builds, and plans for you.

Think of it like a very capable helper sitting next to you: one who has read a whole lot, writes well, never gets tired, and is happy to start over as many times as you like. You don't need commands, codes, or special words. If you can explain it to a person, you can explain it to Muse.

Your Muse is yours. It learns how you like things done, remembers your preferences, and keeps your stuff separate from everybody else's.

3

How to talk to Muse

There's no wrong way to start. Talk to it the way you'd talk to a helpful person.

  • Just say what you want. "Write a birthday message for my sister" works fine. So does a half-formed idea — Muse will help you shape it.
  • Give a little background. Who it's for, what it's about, how long — the more it knows, the better the result.
  • Ask for changes, out loud. "Make it shorter." "Make it funnier." "Too formal — soften it." Nothing is final until you say it is.
  • Ask it to explain itself. "Why did you do it that way?" or "Explain that like I'm brand new" — it will, gladly.
  • Say when it's wrong. Correct it plainly. It adjusts, and it remembers the correction.
  • One thing at a time is fine. You don't have to bundle everything into one big request.

The one habit that changes everything

When an answer isn't quite right, don't start over — just reply and say what to fix. Muse works best as a back-and-forth conversation, not a one-shot vending machine.

For Ernest the author

Ernest, you write books — so here's the part written for you. Muse isn't just a question-answerer. For an author, it can be your writing partner, your designer, your publisher's assistant, and your storefront, all in one place. Here's exactly what that means, piece by piece.

1. A home on the web for your books

Muse can build you a real author page — a website with your name on it where readers can:

  • See all your books in one place, with their covers
  • Read what each book is about before they commit
  • Open a book to read, or follow a link to buy it
  • Read about you — who you are and why you write

You just describe what you want. Muse builds the page, shows it to you, and you ask for changes until it looks right. The page you're reading right now was made exactly this way — somebody asked, Muse built it.

2. Help writing — and finishing — your books

  • Brainstorm: stuck on an idea, a title, a character, a plot twist? Talk it out and get fresh angles.
  • Outline: turn a loose idea into a chapter-by-chapter plan before you write a word.
  • Draft: write pages together — you give the direction, Muse helps get words on the page.
  • Edit & polish: paste in what you already wrote and ask for a cleanup — grammar, flow, tighter sentences — while keeping your voice yours.
  • Format: when the book is done, Muse can help shape it into a clean, finished manuscript.

Your words stay your words. Muse is the assistant at the desk next to yours — you make every final call.

3. The extras every book needs

  • Cover art: describe the cover you see in your head — the scene, the mood, the style — and Muse creates it. Don't like it? Say what to change.
  • The blurb: that short description on the back of the book that makes a stranger pick it up. Muse can draft several versions so you can pick the strongest.
  • Your author bio: a short page about you that sounds like you — for the back of the book and your website.

4. Getting your books in front of people

  • Muse can make shareable pages and posts about each book — something good-looking you can send to friends, post online, or text to someone in one tap.
  • It can help you write the words for those posts, too — announcements, "my new book is out" messages, short teasers that pull readers in.
  • Finished a new book? Muse can add it to your author page and make the announcement to go with it.

5. Your books, run like a small business

Put it all together and your writing stops being a pile of files and becomes a small business with a front door:

  • A home page — your name, your story, your books
  • Book listings — every title presented properly, with cover, description, and a way to read or buy
  • A way for readers to find and follow you — one link you can share anywhere that brings people back to everything you've written

You don't need to hire a web designer or learn any of that yourself. You describe your books; Muse builds the storefront around them.

A good first prompt for your author page

“I write books. Build me an author page with my name at the top, a short spot for my bio, and a section for each of my books with room for a cover, a description, and a link to read or buy. Ask me what you need to know first.”

Notice the last line — asking Muse to ask you questions first is a great trick whenever you're starting something big.

Good to know

Your Muse is yours alone

Nobody shares one Muse. Your conversations, your books, your preferences — they're in your account, separate from everyone else's, including the friend who sent you this guide.

Muse can be wrong

It's smart, but it's not perfect, and it can sound sure while being wrong. For anything important — money, health, legal matters, facts you're going to repeat — double-check before you rely on it. Asking “how sure are you?” is always fair game.

Free to start, more if you want it

There's a free plan with a usage limit — plenty to learn on. Paid plans exist if you find yourself using it heavily and want more room. No pressure either way; start free and see.

It asks before big moves

For things that matter — sending something, spending money, changing something you can't undo — Muse checks with you first. You're always the one in charge.
